  1. State the Cayley-Hamilton Theorem and list two of its primary engineering applications. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    Statement: Every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation. If $|A - \lambda I| = 0$ is $\lambda^n + c_1\lambda^{n-1} + \dots + c_n = 0$, then $A^n + c_1 A^{n-1} + \dots + c_n I = 0$. [1 Mark]

    Applications: (1) Calculation of matrix inverse ($A^{-1}$). (2) Finding higher powers of matrices ($A^n$). [1 Mark]

  2. Using Cayley-Hamilton theorem, find $A^{-1}$ for $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    Char. Eq: $|A - \lambda I| = \lambda^2 - 5 = 0 \implies A^2 - 5I = 0$. [1 Mark]

    Multiply by $A^{-1} \implies 5A^{-1} = A \implies A^{-1} = \frac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$. [1 Mark]

  3. Write the symmetric matrix associated with $Q = 2x_1^2 + 3x_2^2 + 2x_3^2 + 2x_1x_2 - 4x_2x_3$. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    Diagonal entries: $(2, 3, 2)$. Off-diagonal: $a_{12}=a_{21}=1$, $a_{23}=a_{32}=-2$, $a_{13}=a_{31}=0$.

    $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & -2 \\ 0 & -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

  4. Define Rank, Index, and Signature of a quadratic form. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    • Rank ($r$): Number of non-zero terms in canonical form.
    • Index ($s$): Number of positive terms in canonical form.
    • Signature ($2s - r$): Difference between positive and negative terms. [2 Marks]
  5. Classify the nature of $Q = x_1^2 + 2x_2^2 + 3x_3^2$ and state its rank and index. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    All terms positive $(1, 2, 3 > 0)$. Rank $r = 3$, Index $s = 3$. [1 Mark]

    Nature: Positive Definite. [1 Mark]

6. Reduce the quadratic form $Q = 6x_1^2 + 3x_2^2 + 3x_3^2 - 4x_1x_2 - 4x_1x_3 + 2x_2x_3$ to canonical form by orthogonal transformation. Find:
  1. The matrix representation $A$ and its characteristic equation.
  2. The eigenvalues and pairwise orthogonal eigenvectors.
  3. The normalized orthogonal transformation matrix $N$.
  4. The canonical form, rank, index, signature, and nature of the quadratic form.
 [15]

Detailed Solution & Step-by-Step Marking Scheme (15 Marks):

Step 1: Matrix Representation & Characteristic Equation [3 Marks]

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 3 & 1 \\ -2 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$

$S_1 = 6 + 3 + 3 = 12$, $S_2 = 8 + 14 + 14 = 36$, $S_3 = |A| = 32$.

Char. Eq: $\lambda^3 - 12\lambda^2 + 36\lambda - 32 = 0$.

Step 2: Eigenvalues & Pairwise Orthogonal Eigenvectors [4 Marks]

Solving $(\lambda - 8)(\lambda - 2)^2 = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = 8, \lambda_2 = 2, \lambda_3 = 2$.

• For $\lambda = 8$: $(A - 8I)X = 0 \implies X_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}^T$.

• For $\lambda = 2$: $(A - 2I)X = 0 \implies 4x_1 - 2x_2 - 2x_3 = 0 \implies 2x_1 - x_2 - x_3 = 0$.

Choose $X_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}^T$. Let $X_3 = \begin{pmatrix} l & m & n \end{pmatrix}^T$.

Using orthogonality $X_1 \cdot X_3 = 0$ and $X_2 \cdot X_3 = 0 \implies X_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}^T$.

Step 3: Normalized Matrix $N$ & Orthogonal Transformation [3 Marks]

Lengths: $|X_1| = \sqrt{6}, |X_2| = \sqrt{2}, |X_3| = \sqrt{3}$.

$$N = \begin{pmatrix} -2/\sqrt{6} & 0 & 1/\sqrt{3} \\ 1/\sqrt{6} & 1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{3} \\ 1/\sqrt{6} & -1/\sqrt{2} & 1/\sqrt{3} \end{pmatrix}$$

Transformation $X = NY$ transforms $Q$ to $Y^T D Y$ where $D = \text{diag}(8, 2, 2)$.

Step 4: Canonical Form & Properties [5 Marks]

Canonical Form: $8y_1^2 + 2y_2^2 + 2y_3^2$. [2M]

  • Rank ($r$) = $3$ (All 3 terms present) [1M]
  • Index ($s$) = $3$ (All 3 terms positive) [1M]
  • Signature ($2s - r$) = $2(3) - 3 = 3$
  • Nature: Positive Definite (Since all eigenvalues $> 0$) [1M]

  1. If characteristic equation of $A$ is $\lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 = 0$, express $A^3$ in terms of $A$ and $I$ using C-H theorem. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    $A^2 - 4A + 3I = 0 \implies A^2 = 4A - 3I$. [1 Mark]

    $$A^3 = 4A^2 - 3A = 4(4A - 3I) - 3A = 13A - 12I \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

  2. Find $A^{-1}$ using Cayley-Hamilton theorem for $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    Char. Eq: $\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 7 = 0 \implies A^2 - 5A + 7I = 0$. [1 Mark]

    $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{7}(5I - A) = \begin{pmatrix} 2/7 & -1/7 \\ 1/7 & 3/7 \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

  3. Express $Q(x,y,z) = x^2 + 5y^2 + z^2 + 2xy - 6yz + 4zx$ in symmetric matrix form. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    Diagonal: $(1, 5, 1)$, Off-diagonal: $a_{12}=1, a_{23}=-3, a_{13}=2$.

    $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 & -3 \\ 2 & -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

  4. State eigenvalue conditions for a quadratic form to be (i) Positive Semi-Definite, (ii) Indefinite. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    (i) Positive Semi-Definite: All $\lambda_i \ge 0$ with at least one $\lambda_k = 0$. [1 Mark]

    (ii) Indefinite: Eigenvalues contain both positive and negative values. [1 Mark]

  5. Convert $Q = -x_1^2 - 2x_2^2 - 5x_3^2$ to canonical form and state its nature, rank, and index. [2]

    Solution & Scheme:

    Canonical Form: $-y_1^2 - 2y_2^2 - 5y_3^2$.

    Rank $r = 3$, Index $s = 0$. [1 Mark]

    Nature: Negative Definite. [1 Mark]

6. Reduce the quadratic form $Q = 2x_1^2 + 5x_2^2 + 3x_3^2 + 4x_1x_2$ to canonical form by orthogonal transformation. Find:
  1. The matrix representation $A$ and its characteristic equation.
  2. The eigenvalues and pairwise orthogonal eigenvectors.
  3. The normalized orthogonal transformation matrix $N$.
  4. The canonical form, rank, index, signature, and nature of the quadratic form.
 [15]

Detailed Solution & Step-by-Step Marking Scheme (15 Marks):

Step 1: Matrix Representation & Characteristic Equation [3 Marks]

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$

$S_1 = 2 + 5 + 3 = 10$, $S_2 = (10-4) + 15 + 6 = 27$, $S_3 = |A| = 3(10 - 4) = 18$.

Char. Eq: $\lambda^3 - 10\lambda^2 + 27\lambda - 18 = 0$.

Step 2: Eigenvalues & Pairwise Orthogonal Eigenvectors [4 Marks]

Factorizing: $(\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 3)(\lambda - 6) = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = 1, \lambda_2 = 3, \lambda_3 = 6$.

• For $\lambda = 1$: $(A - I)X = 0 \implies X_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^T$.

• For $\lambda = 3$: $(A - 3I)X = 0 \implies X_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}^T$.

• For $\lambda = 6$: $(A - 6I)X = 0 \implies X_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^T$.

(Note: $X_1 \cdot X_2 = 0, X_2 \cdot X_3 = 0, X_1 \cdot X_3 = 0$ verified).

Step 3: Normalized Matrix $N$ & Orthogonal Transformation [3 Marks]

Lengths: $|X_1| = \sqrt{5}, |X_2| = 1, |X_3| = \sqrt{5}$.

$$N = \begin{pmatrix} -2/\sqrt{5} & 0 & 1/\sqrt{5} \\ 1/\sqrt{5} & 0 & 2/\sqrt{5} \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

Transformation $X = NY$ satisfies $N^T A N = D = \text{diag}(1, 3, 6)$.

Step 4: Canonical Form & Properties [5 Marks]

Canonical Form: $1y_1^2 + 3y_2^2 + 6y_3^2$. [2M]

  • Rank ($r$) = $3$ [1M]
  • Index ($s$) = $3$ [1M]
  • Signature ($2s - r$) = $2(3) - 3 = 3$
  • Nature: Positive Definite (Since all eigenvalues $> 0$) [1M]
